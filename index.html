01Turns repeated work into automation that outlives him.

Daily platform health checks used to be a manual click-through across two systems. I rewrote them as PowerShell and Ansible tasks hitting the Panorama and Ivanti Connect Secure REST APIs, handled auth and partial-failure states, and documented the runbook so anyone on the team can run or extend it.

That's roughly 3.5 hours a week returned permanently — and it compounds, because the next check added costs minutes instead of an hour.

Evidence — ~40 minutes saved per daily cycle. NYC OTI, 2024 to present. Ask me to walk through the playbook structure and how I handled API auth and failure states.

02Learns unfamiliar platforms in production, in weeks not quarters.

QRadar, Palo Alto Panorama and Ansible were all new to me in January 2024. Within two months I was making production changes on all three — under change control, with senior review until I no longer needed it. AWS I taught myself off-hours and confirmed with the Solutions Architect exam rather than claiming it on a resume. Ansible I picked up specifically because I wanted to ship the health-check automation and needed a tool that would let me.

I'd rather be measured on that than promise it — hand me your stack and check in at 45 days.

Evidence — three unfamiliar platforms to daily operator inside one role · AWS SAA and CCP, self-taught, Credly-verified · three Palo Alto instructor-led courses applied on live infrastructure.

03Trusted with infrastructure a city depends on.

Firewall policy, DNS records, proxy configuration and remote access for multiple New York City agencies — an environment where a bad rule change is a public-facing outage, not a rollback in staging.

Evidence — NYC Office of Technology & Innovation, January 2024 to present. References available on request.

04Writes the incident down so it doesn't happen twice.

I respond to live outages, write the root cause analysis, and drive the corrective action to closure — the part most people skip once the alert goes green.

I also repaired QRadar ingestion and parsing gaps that were silently costing detection coverage. A rule is worthless if its log source stopped shipping three weeks ago, and nobody files a ticket for that because nothing visibly breaks.

Evidence — RCA authorship at NYC OTI. Happy to talk through a sanitised example end to end.
