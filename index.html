01 — Assessment6 claims · evidence attached

What the file says

Every claim below opens to show the evidence behind it. Nothing here is asserted without something you can verify or ask me about in an interview.

01Turns repeated work into automation that outlives him.

Our daily platform health checks used to be a manual click-through across two systems. I'd never worked with APIs before, so I read the Palo Alto and Ivanti documentation, introduced Ansible to the team, and built 3–4 PowerShell and Ansible checks that pull the status for us. Then I wrote the runbook so anyone on the team can run it.

That's roughly 3.5 hours a week returned to the team — and every new check I add costs minutes instead of an hour.

Evidence — ~40 minutes saved per daily cycle. NYC OTI, 2024 to present. Ask me to walk through how I built it and what I learned along the way.

02Learns unfamiliar platforms in production, in weeks not quarters.

QRadar, Palo Alto Panorama and Ansible were all new to me in January 2024. Within two months I was making production changes on all three — under change control, with senior review until I no longer needed it. AWS I taught myself off-hours and confirmed with the Solutions Architect exam. Ansible I picked up specifically because I wanted to ship the health-check automation.

I'd rather be measured on that than promise it — hand me your stack and check in at 45 days.

Evidence — three unfamiliar platforms to daily operator inside one role · AWS SAA and CCP, self-taught, Credly-verified · three Palo Alto instructor-led courses applied on live infrastructure.

03Learns your stack before the first day, not after.

If your team runs something I haven't used in production — Elastic, Sentinel, Proofpoint, Kubernetes, whatever it is — I'll spend the time between offer and start date getting hands-on with it: vendor training, labs, docs, a home setup if that's what it takes.

I'm not going to pretend I already know everything. What I can promise is that you won't be teaching me from zero on day one.

Evidence — see claim 02: QRadar, Panorama and Ansible to production-ready in about two months. Weekly TryHackMe practice on top of the day job.

04Trusted with infrastructure a city depends on.

Firewall policy, DNS records, proxy configuration and remote access for multiple New York City agencies — an environment where a bad rule change is a public-facing outage, not a rollback in staging.

Evidence — NYC Office of Technology & Innovation, January 2024 to present. Talk to my current and past supervisors — see references.

05Writes the incident down so it doesn't happen twice.

I respond to live outages, write the root cause analysis, and drive the corrective action to closure — the part most people skip once the alert goes green.

I also helped fix QRadar ingestion and parsing gaps that were silently costing detection coverage. A rule is worthless if its log source stopped shipping three weeks ago, and nobody files a ticket for that because nothing visibly breaks.

Evidence — RCA authorship at NYC OTI. Happy to talk through a sanitised example end to end.

06Builds security tools nobody asked him to build.

OSINTVault is a fully offline, backend-free OSINT workspace for analysts who can't send case data to somebody else's cloud. PROTECT is a cybercrime reporting platform I led and shipped, designed so ordinary New Yorkers can report a threat without knowing the vocabulary.

Evidence — github.com/w3bcooki3 · 1st place, John Jay College cybersecurity hackathon · 3rd place, AWS hackathon at Queensborough.
